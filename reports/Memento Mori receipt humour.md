# Make mortality earn its receipt space

**The best Memento Mori receipt needs one time counter and several different kinds of reversal, not the same number repeated as weeks, Mondays and weekends.** This research scans **42 distinct primary-source URLs** across commercial objects, historical collections, author texts, contemporary comedy and practical death-administration resources. It is a broad, researched selection, not an exhaustive catalogue of the internet. The strongest material treats an ordinary system—warranties, shopping carts, account settings, luggage or customer service—as unable to process the fact that a person is finite. Below are **60 deduplicated label/value candidates newly written for this project**, including a strongest-ten selection; they are creative proposals, not jokes copied from those sources or a claim that similar wording has never existed. Historical facts and product mechanisms are cited separately from that original writing. The app/PDF corrections—larger name, selectable printing/birthday date, one weekly counter, and **“THANKS FOR LIVING, COME AGAIN!”**—are independent of this candidate bank.

## Objects make an abstract deadline ordinary

The week-grid format works because it turns time into finite stock. Tim Urban's original essay uses a 90-year illustrative grid, while Kurzgesagt's calendar adds the physical ritual of marking boxes and recording events. Mementoweek adds a game-like status view. These are three versions of a mechanism, not three different predictions that belong together on a receipt: the chosen lifespan is an assumption, and a box is a unit of attention. One such anchor can do the work; the next row should change the subject. ([Tim Urban](https://waitbutwhy.com/2014/05/life-weeks.html), [Kurzgesagt](https://shop-us.kurzgesagt.org/products/lifespan-calendar-poster), [Mementoweek](https://mementoweek.com/en/about))

A different mechanism is interruption. WeCroak's developer listing describes five randomly timed daily reminders, allowing mortality to arrive while something mundane is happening. Death Clock instead offers a projected lifespan influenced by personal inputs and frames it as part of a health dashboard. The useful design contrast is between a supposedly final deadline and the urge to optimise it. **Those are vendor descriptions, not evidence of clinical accuracy, psychological benefit, or a basis for predicting a customer's death date.** ([WeCroak developer listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en&id=com.kkitcreations.wecroak), [Death Clock developer listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/body-tracker-death-clock/id6499554412), [Death Clock company account](https://www.deathclock.co/about))

Coins convert the reminder into something a hand can find in a pocket. Daily Stoic uses skull, flower and hourglass imagery; EDC Reminder Coins similarly makes mortality part of an everyday-carry routine. Our creative inference is that a spendable-looking coin cannot buy more time. It suggests account, credit and redemption jokes without reproducing either maker's artwork or sales copy. ([Daily Stoic](https://store.dailystoic.com/collections/gift-guide/products/memento-mori), [EDC Reminder Coins](https://www.edcremindercoins.com/en-gb/collections/stoic-coins/products/memento-mori-coin))

Watches supply several distinct tensions. Augustin places real sand alongside diamonds; Markwell hides a skull on the caseback. Science Museum examples conceal a watch inside a skull, including an object whose royal ownership story the museum qualifies rather than authenticates. A clock battery outliving a warranty, a private reminder under a polished exterior, or a skull arriving with timekeeping equipment is more specific than another remaining-week total. ([Augustin](https://augustinwatches.com/en/watch/memento-mori/), [Markwell](https://www.markwellwatches.com/products/stainless-masonic), [Science Museum skull watch and qualified attribution](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8558097/memento-mori-watch-in-the-form-of-a-skull-known-as-the-mary-queen-of-scots-watch-skull-watch-verge-movement), [Science Museum concealed pocket watch](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co77704/pocket-watch-concealed-in-a-memento-mori-pocket-watch))

Fragile and wearable objects broaden the vocabulary further. NUDE places skull forms into glassware and domestic objects, making even a death reminder something that can break. Trendhim's account shows a maker adapting skulls, hourglasses and decay into wearable design. These sources support fragility and daily use as mechanisms; they do not grant permission to reproduce branded designs. A commercial life-jar/marble-counter source was not verified strongly enough to include. ([NUDE](https://eu.nudeglass.com/collections/memento-mori/), [Trendhim's design account](https://www.trendhim.ie/articles/912/how-we-designed-the-memento-mori-collection))

## Older stories offer different punchlines than a countdown

Vanitas provides an inventory of things that do not last: an exhausted candle, a bubble, a fading flower, an hourglass and music that ends. The Museum of Sepulchral Culture explains this symbolic vocabulary, and the Warhol Museum explicitly connects mortality with irony and humour. The receipt can borrow the general logic of an inventory without pretending that every row needs a quantity. **CANDLE WARRANTY / VOID WHEN LIT** is original retail phrasing for an old motif. ([Museum of Sepulchral Culture](https://www.sepulkralmuseum.de/en/sammlungen/einblicke-in-die-sammlung/vanitas-stillleben/), [Andy Warhol Museum](https://www.warhol.org/exhibition/vanitas/))

A skull can work through recognition or surprise. Hals's young man holding a skull gives a direct reminder; Holbein's distorted skull in *The Ambassadors* becomes legible from an oblique viewpoint amid displays of learning and wealth. That contrast suggests a perspective gag, not a licence to treat every object in the painting as an identical death symbol. ([National Gallery: Hals](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/frans-hals-young-man-holding-a-skull-vanitas), [National Gallery: Holbein](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/hans-holbein-the-younger-the-ambassadors))

The Dance of Death supplies social levelling. Library accounts describe figures of different religious and secular ranks entering the same procession; the Met's object record grounds the Holbein/Lützelburger prints in identifiable historical objects. The Morgan describes Death interrupting daily activities rather than merely waiting at the end of a queue. Together these mechanisms suggest a VIP exit with the same door, premium membership that does not change mortality, and unfinished business left in a cart. **The Morgan description was available through its search-index text; direct access returned 403, so that access path is weaker than the opened collection records.** ([University of Toronto](https://collections.library.utoronto.ca/explore/hollar/books/dancedeath), [SMU: Dance of Death before Holbein](https://bridwell.omeka.net/exhibits/show/danceofdeath/beforeholbein), [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/360011), [Morgan Library](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/online/holbein/images-of-death))

The Roman triumph anecdote needs restraint. Tertullian reports a reminder of a ruler's humanity; this does not establish the entire popular story of a slave continuously whispering a fixed Latin formula at every triumph. Marcus Aurelius and Seneca are stronger direct sources for attention to present choices and the accounting of time. They make ethical arguments, not personal actuarial forecasts. Our receipt can use an ego check or nonrefundable worry without attaching an invented quotation to an emperor. ([Tertullian, chapter 33](https://www.tertullian.org/articles/reeve_apology.htm), [Marcus Aurelius, book II](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.2.two.html), [Seneca, primary text translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/64576/64576-h/64576-h.htm))

Instruction manuals and books offer another direction. *Ars Moriendi* belongs to a specific late-medieval Christian tradition of preparing for death; Bosch's *Death and the Miser* connects dying with worldly attachment. Neither should be flattened into a universal religious claim. Franklin's proposed literary epitaph compares a printer's body with a book and imagines a corrected edition, but it is not simply the wording of his actual gravestone. These support jokes about unread exit instructions, a body going out of print, and an author unavailable for revision. ([SMU: Ars Moriendi](https://bridwell.omeka.net/exhibits/show/worshipdevotion/arsmoriendi), [National Gallery of Art: Bosch](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/41645-death-and-miser), [Library of Congress: Franklin context](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/franklin/franklin-epitaph.html), [Franklin primary text reproduction](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36338/36338-h/36338-h.htm))

## Modern humour works through administration and unfinished life

Contemporary comedy often gives the cosmic ordinary work. The official *Mort* synopsis places Death in an employment/apprenticeship frame. Rob Wilkins's remembrance of Terry Pratchett and the official biography connect loss with everyday communication and a remarkably physical instruction for destroying unfinished writing. Monty Python's official *Dead Parrot* upload is a useful comparator for a customer-complaint frame. **These are mechanisms to learn from, not dialogue, characters or recognizable routines to reproduce.** The video was accessible through indexed official material rather than a usable full transcript. ([Mort](https://www.terrypratchettbooks.com/books/mort/), [Official remembrance](https://terrypratchett.com/news/remembering-sir-terry-pratchett-10-years-on/), [Official biography](https://terrypratchett.com/terry-pratchett/), [Official Monty Python upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TaFDzTzKAT0))

Real planning resources show why paperwork is a fertile vocabulary. Chanel Reynolds uses candid language around practical preparation; the Order of the Good Death addresses both unusual household questions and the work of recording wishes and arranging a handover. The receipt can make a joke about an unread form while leaving genuine legal or medical guidance outside the product. Death Cafe's tea-and-cake setting supplies a useful tonal counterweight: mortality does not require every line to be bleak. ([Get Your Shit Together](https://getyourshittogether.org/checklist/), [Order of the Good Death podcast](https://www.orderofthegooddeath.com/podcast/), [End-of-life planning](https://www.orderofthegooddeath.com/resources/end-of-life-planning/), [Death Cafe](https://deathcafe.com/what/))

Digital accounts supply specific modern reversals. Apple's Legacy Contact has defined access conditions and exclusions; it does not hand over every password or purchase. Google's Inactive Account Manager can notify people or share selected data after configured inactivity. Meta's memorialization announcement demonstrates the tension between automatic social prompts and grief, but its 2019 publication is historical evidence, not current setup instructions. Candidate jokes about drafts, permissions and inactive accounts are original inventions, not factual claims that all platforms behave the same way. ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102631), [Google Account Help](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/3036546?hl=en), [Meta's historical announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2019/04/updates-to-memorialization/amp/))

Personalized funeral objects and mourning jewellery show the value of the individual. Creative Coffins expresses identity through recognizable interests; London Museum describes names, dates and personal details in mourning jewellery. For this receipt, the name should occupy a meaningful place in the design. The wit surrounds a person; it should not turn that person into an anonymous row of counts. ([Creative Coffins](https://www.creativecoffins.co.uk/our-coffins/), [London Museum](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/blog/mourning-jewellery-love-loss/))

## Ten lines deserve the first print test

These are **editorial selections from the original bank below**, not verified audience favourites. They span distinct mechanisms so the joke does not become ten versions of “time is running out.”

**EXPIRY DATE — NOT ON THE LABEL** opens clearly and admits uncertainty instead of predicting a death date.

**EXTENDED WARRANTY — NICE TRY** belongs naturally beside the existing playful warranty selector.

**SKULL — FACTORY INSTALLED** makes a concrete object carry the joke.

**VIP EXIT — SAME DOOR** supplies a status reversal rather than another clock.

**LAST WORDS — DRAFT UNSAVED** gives a small modern failure irreversible weight.

**UNFINISHED BUSINESS — LEFT IN CART** fits the shopping receipt and leaves room for the reader's own interpretation.

**PERSONAL BELONGINGS — NOW OTHER PEOPLE'S** reverses ownership in one short turn.

**FUNERAL PLAYLIST — NO SKIP BUTTON** offers an everyday frustration in an imagined final setting.

**LEGACY FORMAT — MOSTLY ANECDOTES** adds warmth and changes the emotional register.

**UNMADE PHONE CALLS — STILL POSSIBLE** is the best closing beat because the reader can act on it now.

Use roughly six to eight new lines in a single edition, not this entire bank. A coherent short story is stronger than a dense joke catalogue: a warranty fails, a draft remains unfinished, possessions change hands, stories survive, and a phone call is still possible. The existing counts of future funerals or graveyard visits must not be presented as predictions established by this research; no source here supports such personal statistical estimates.

## Let the reader finish the story

The strongest receipt leaves a little interpretive space. It does not have to explain why a skull is factory-installed or why an unfinished message belongs in a cart; the reader supplies that connection. A varied sequence also makes the personal name and date more consequential: this is a record issued to somebody, not a collection of interchangeable mortality slogans. Keep one numerical anchor if desired, then let objects, status, possessions, digital life and relationships take turns.

This bank is ready for editorial selection and print-size testing, not automatic insertion of every line. It contains no evidence that a particular joke improves sales, no validated prediction of anyone's remaining life, and no authorization to reproduce source artwork or protected routines. The report itself performs no purchase, account action, public publication or checkout activation. Preserve the existing requirement for owner approval before any public live release.
